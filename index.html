T3R:

Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models

We present T3R, a training-free framework that tackles two complementary sources of VLA redundancy. The first stage removes irrelevant visual patches before decoder entry. The second stage redirects action attention toward instruction-critical evidence, improving efficiency and robustness without retraining.

Abstract

Vision-Language-Action models process hundreds of visual tokens at every decision step, increasing latency while allowing irrelevant context to distract action prediction under distribution shift. T3R addresses two complementary forms of redundancy: spatially irrelevant visual tokens and misallocated attention over the retained sequence.

T3R is fully training-free. Stage 1 uses instruction-conditioned segmentation to prune visual patches before decoder entry. Stage 2 uses Integrated Gradients to identify instruction-salient tokens and applies an additive attention bias in the middle decoder layers. Across OpenVLA-OFT, CogACT, and π0.5, the method improves the accuracy-efficiency frontier without changing model weights.

Method Details

Segmentation-guided pruning

The retained token budget adapts to scene difficulty rather than applying one fixed spatial pattern. Because pruning occurs before decoder entry, every language-model layer processes the shorter sequence.

Saliency-guided attention

Five effective IG steps identify instruction tokens that matter to the predicted action. Middle-layer biasing recovers six success points over pruning alone on LIBERO-Spatial.

Quantitative Results

On LIBERO-Spatial, T3R prunes 85% of scene-camera tokens while retaining 96% success, one point below the unpruned model and far above competing methods at the same reduction.

Limitations

The experiments are simulation-based. Benefits are strongest in multi-camera settings, where views provide redundant evidence. Aggressive irreversible pruning can be less effective in single-camera or multi-phase tasks, and segmentation errors can remove necessary evidence.

On LIBERO-Object, for example, T3R underperforms TeamVLA when similarity splits between a source object and a visually dominant destination. These cases motivate phase-aware re-localization and reversible token selection.
